Reaching millions, and earning from it

Growth v1.0 12 November 2026, with handbook v1.21. The production steps are on the content pipeline; this page decides what we make, how long, in which format, where it goes and how it earns.

Your brief: videos that reach millions of views and earn, India first, in English and Hindi, explaining the news without taking sides, at one long film and 5–7 Shorts a week. Here is the plan, with the numbers behind it.

The plan in one paragraph

Shorts bring the audience; long films bring the money. A million Short views earn about what 10,000–30,000 long-form views earn. So every week we make one 8–12 minute film and 5–7 Shorts that point to it. The money engine is "What if you…", with Gappu as you, the format behind a channel that earned $27.3K in 28 days. Topics come from three engines: the "why" behind this week's news, the first principles of everyday things, and imagination. We switch on one source of money at a time, as each gate opens. What sets us apart is what cheap AI channels can't copy: truth with sources, first principles, India first, our own characters, and a Lab where the viewer plays with the machine they just watched.

How YouTube decides what to show

YouTube doesn't push a video out. It pulls a video in for each viewer when it predicts that viewer will watch it and be glad they did official. It names clicks, watch time, survey answers, shares, likes, dislikes and "Not interested" as signals. Only watch time that viewers rate 4 or 5 stars in its surveys counts as "valued watch time".

  1. Impressionsthe topic
  2. Clicktitle and thumbnail
  3. First 30 sthe promise, delivered
  4. Minutes watchedno drag
  5. Glad they watchedlikes, shares, the next video, coming back
  6. Wider circlesshown to more people like them

A video goes viral when it keeps winning each wider circle.

Where views come from

Browse (the home page): packaging and satisfaction. Suggested (next to similar videos): where news-wave films ride. Search: the words people type, for years. Shorts feed: viewed vs swiped away, % viewed, loops, shares.

Test & Compare

Up to three thumbnails, titles or both. The winner is chosen by watch-time share, not clicks, so a misleading thumbnail loses even when it gets clicked. official

Hype

Viewers get three free hypes a week for long videos under seven days old; hyped videos climb a country leaderboard. Open to partner channels with 500–500,000 subscribers: ask for it after Gate 1. official

Clickbait in India

Since 18 Dec 2024 YouTube removes videos whose title or thumbnail promises what the video doesn't deliver, especially on news, starting in India. Our rule: the promise is delivered in 15 seconds. official

Seven patterns behind them

  1. One idea per video, nameable in 2–5 words.
  2. The viewer is inside the story: "you".
  3. A visible counter or progress gives a reason to stay.
  4. Every beat bigger than the last.
  5. Series parts that end on an open question.
  6. One recognisable look, so the thumbnail is known before it's read.
  7. A next step at the end: the next part, the Lab, the product.

News: explain it, never take sides

In Context moves from "slow news" to timely, not breaking: we publish when the questions peak (Shorts within 24–72 hours, the long film within about a week), and every In Context rule stays.

  • The "why" layer, not the headline: the mechanism underneath, and what it means for an Indian viewer.
  • Every claim classed and dated "as of"; corrections pinned.
  • Each side's strongest case in its own words. No verdict, no election predictions.
  • Faith and epics: "the text says", "historians say", "many believe"; never "debunked", never mocking.
  • War and tragedy: maps, mechanisms and consequences; no graphic footage, casualty images or fear thumbnails.
  • Allegations: only what is on record (filings, regulators, courts), labelled "alleged", with the reply.
  • Security: what goes wrong and why, never how.
  • No film or TV footage: we draw it. India's "fair dealing" is narrower than US fair use.

Engagement: keep them watching, bring them back

Shorts open

Frame 1 is the post: subject and motion, no logo. The question or stake in 1–2 seconds; the payoff by ~70 %; the last frame loops into the first.

Long films open

The promise said and shown in 15 seconds. The logo sting moves after the cold open. Aim: 70 % still watching at 30 seconds.

Shape: promise → stakes for "you" → escalate (every beat bigger) → a re-hook every 60–90 seconds → payoff → the next question, handed to the end screen (the next part, 20 seconds).

Devices

  • A counter on screen: day 1 → day 30, ₹ spent, km, °C.
  • "Pause and guess" before a reveal: the Lab's predict → act → consequence.
  • "You decide": a Community poll chooses what Gappu does next episode.
  • "You asked": an idea from the comments, credited to the commenter's handle.
  • Bindu and Gappu in every animated film; numbered series; one recurring sign-off question.

Fun, not drag

Something new every 5–10 seconds (a fact, an image, a joke, a turn). No throat-clearing, no recaps. Humour from Gappu's reactions; the music changes at each act; never faster than ~190 words a minute.

Cadence

Shorts 5–7 a week at the same time; the long film the same day and time each week. A daily series only for Shorts that take under an hour each (loop shorts, a one-idea-a-day series). A daily long series isn't sustainable for one person with a job.

"Subscribe to watch more"

Ask once, after the first payoff, with a reason ("Part 2: what happens on day 31"). Never hold the payoff back on a public video. After Gate 1, members get parts early.

Beyond the video

Ask for Hype after Gate 1; quizzes in Community posts; Lab challenges ("can you land Vikram?") with a leaderboard; reply to comments in the first hour after a publish.

Our seven moats

  1. Truth in an age of slop: evidence labelled, sources shown, corrections kept.
  2. First principles: you'll understand it, not just hear it.
  3. India first: the global story told through an Indian viewer's money, city, history and future.
  4. Our own world: Bindu and Gappu, our type per series, our organ score.
  5. The Lab: the viewer plays with the machine they just watched. No other channel has that.
  6. Two languages on one video.
  7. Recognisable packaging: a thumbnail system per series (for "What if you…": Gappu, a two-word line, one bright object).

The human behind it: a five-second "I make these" moment or a voice note in a Community post builds trust. Your call; not required.

Money, one stage at a time

You asked whether to switch on every source of money at once, or one by one. One by one. Each stage needs the audience the previous one built, and splitting effort early takes it away from the one thing that matters first: better films.

StageTurn onIgnore for now
Now → Gate 1views, retention, packaging; a Lab link in every descriptionsponsors, memberships, products
Gate 1Super Thanks; one membership tier (early parts, a vote on the next episode, behind the scenes); ask for Hypemore tiers
Gate 2ads; mid-rolls on 8+ minute films at natural breaks; the Hindi track on every long film
Gate 3sponsors: a media kit (audience, countries, retention) and a rate card from our own views; one 60–90 s integration per filmbrands that don't fit
Gate 4memberships with real value; Instagram subscriptions; X rewards; Lab licences for schools
Gate 5paid Lab experiences, courses, workshops, posters, books

Sponsor rules

Always disclosed (YouTube's paid-promotion box and India's ASCI influencer rules). Only brands that fit; never in news, health or children's topics. Never real-money gaming (banned in India, advertising included, by the Online Gaming Act 2025), betting, crypto schemes or "get rich" products. Reported rates at ~100K subscribers: ₹50,000–3 lakh a video reported; ours will come from our own average views.

Money admin

AdSense pays in dollars. Submit the US tax form in AdSense, so earnings from US viewers aren't withheld at the highest rate (check the current rate for India). Talk to a chartered accountant about income tax and GST once revenue starts.
